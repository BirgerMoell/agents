# CGI Responsible AI

Source: https://www.cgi.com/en/artificial-intelligence/responsible-ai
Retrieved: 2026-09-27

## Purpose and risks

CGI describes responsible AI as defining principles, governing their use, and putting
them into practice so that AI outcomes are safe, reliable, and ethical. Risks associated
with AI include limited transparency, privacy and copyright infringement, security
threats, and reputational or financial harm. CGI argues that governance must balance value
creation with risk mitigation.

## Three pillars

CGI defines three pillars of Responsible AI. Robust means reliable and safe outcomes,
legal compliance, rigorous design, development, testing and operations, and supporting
privacy and security practices. Ethical means aligning AI goals with human values,
fairness and inclusion, while producing sustainable benefits for people, society and the
environment. Trustworthy means using explainable and interpretable models, providing
transparent inputs and outcomes, and accepting accountability for controlled AI systems.

## Governance and implementation

CGI's approach includes scientific-research principles, enforceable guidelines, best
practices, and an AI risk matrix. CGI says responsible AI should be built into strategy
and delivery from the beginning rather than added afterward. It works with clients on
appropriate oversight, governance, model-risk assessment, and model selection. CGI is a
signatory of Canada's Voluntary Code of Conduct for Artificial Intelligence and the EU AI
Act Pledge within the European Commission's AI Pact.
